# Information Architecture

Use IA to organize user-facing concepts and destinations so people can predict where information lives and how to reach it.

## Core concerns

- organization
- hierarchy
- taxonomy/classification
- labeling
- relationships
- findability
- orientation
- task coverage

## Hierarchy rules

Good hierarchies tend to have:

- understandable parent/child ownership
- siblings at comparable abstraction levels
- labels that are distinct without needing explanation
- depth justified by real containment/context
- enough breadth to expose major product areas without overwhelming users

There is no universal ideal depth/breadth number. Judge by recognition, task cost, and domain complexity.

## Classification tests

When grouping destinations or objects, ask:

- Would users predict this item belongs here?
- Is this grouping based on user/domain meaning or implementation ownership?
- Are two different organizing principles mixed at the same level?
- Does one category become a dumping ground such as "More" or "Tools"?

## Labeling

Prefer labels that:

- use stable domain language
- distinguish siblings clearly
- describe what users will find, not internal team ownership
- remain sensible across entry points

Avoid unnecessary synonyms for the same concept.

## Traceability

A primary destination should normally trace to at least one established intention/capability.

A primary intention should have a plausible route through the IA.

Use traceability as a challenge mechanism, not as bureaucracy.
