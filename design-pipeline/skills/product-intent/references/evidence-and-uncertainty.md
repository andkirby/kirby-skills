# Evidence and Uncertainty

The skill must be useful when evidence is incomplete without pretending uncertainty does not exist.

## Evidence ladder

From stronger to weaker for a specific product claim:

1. Direct observed behavior or validated project evidence.
2. Repeated user/customer evidence relevant to the target context.
3. Explicit product/business decision from a canonical source.
4. Existing product behavior or support patterns that imply a need.
5. Domain-informed inference.
6. Unsupported assumption.

The exact ordering can vary by context; the important rule is to label what kind of evidence a claim rests on.

## Confidence is not a fake score

Prefer labels such as:

- established
- supported
- inferred
- assumed
- unknown

Do not invent percentages.

## Escalate uncertainty when it changes

- primary actor
- product value proposition
- major capability set
- MVP boundary
- information architecture
- permissions/ownership model

Do not block downstream work over small wording or low-impact uncertainty.

## Minimum validation principle

If an assumption is consequential and cheaply testable, recommend the smallest test that could change the decision. Examples:

- inspect support/search data
- compare existing workflows
- ask a focused user question
- test a hypothesis with a lightweight prototype

Do not turn every assumption into a research project.
